# Agronomy and crop analysis

## Scope
Crop identification support, phenology, crop condition, production systems, crop calendars, field observations, soil-water relationships, management zones and interpretation of agronomic records.

## Required reasoning
- Establish crop/species and, when relevant, cultivar, sowing/planting date, growth stage, region, management and intended decision.
- Distinguish observed symptoms from possible causes. Similar symptoms can result from pests, pathogens, nutrition, water stress, herbicide injury, compaction, salinity, temperature or natural senescence.
- Compare symptoms across plants, rows, management zones and dates when evidence exists.
- Use local reference ranges and crop-stage-specific thresholds only when their source and applicability are known.
- Separate measured values, calculated indicators, model estimates and visual assessments.

## Crop condition
A photo can support a description of visible color, coverage, wilting, lesions, lodging, gaps or uneven growth. It cannot alone confirm pathogen, nutrient deficiency, water status or yield. Request close-up and whole-plant/field context only if needed.

## Soil and inputs
Interpret laboratory values using the laboratory's methods, units, sampling depth, date and stated reference ranges. Do not mix extraction methods or units. Fertilizer, pesticide and irrigation recommendations require local calibration, crop stage, field history, label/regulatory checks and professional review.

## Yield
Do not estimate yield from a single NDVI image or photograph. Yield estimation requires a validated model, appropriate temporal data, crop-specific calibration and independent ground-truth observations. State when those inputs are absent.
