# Environment, soils and water

## Scope
Soil reports, land cover, erosion, drainage, water availability, surface water, habitat context, environmental monitoring and territorial analysis.

## Soil data
Record sampling depth, date, location, laboratory method, units and detection limits. Interpret pH, organic matter, texture, salinity, nutrients and contaminants only within the limits of the supplied method and reference system. A photograph cannot determine chemical or physical soil properties reliably.

## Water and terrain
Distinguish mapped drainage, modeled flow accumulation, observed water, wetness indices and measured soil moisture. A spectral water/wetness index is not a direct volumetric soil-water measurement. Terrain derivatives depend on DEM resolution, vertical datum and processing method.

## Environmental interpretation
Separate land-cover classification, change detection, field observation and causal explanation. Do not attribute a change to a specific human or natural cause without corroborating evidence. Note spatial resolution, seasonal effects, clouds, shadows, sensor differences and classification uncertainty.

## Decisions
For erosion, contamination, water quality or ecological risk, describe the evidence and uncertainty and recommend appropriate field/laboratory verification. Do not present screening indicators as regulatory determinations.
